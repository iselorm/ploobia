How anything is learned here

Four moves, in every single region.

Ploobia is not a textbook with buttons on it. Every region is a working model — change an input and the consequences propagate, whether or not that is convenient for the lesson.

01 / CHANGE

Change something

Move the sun, raise the temperature, add a tributary, pour in another electron. Nothing is on rails.

02 / MEASURE

Measure what happens

Every region carries real instruments — a gauge, a stopwatch, a bubble counter, a shell meter — and your readings go into a table and a live graph.

03 / EXPLAIN

Explain why

Then the hard part, and the whole point: a claim, the evidence you actually recorded, and the reasoning that joins them.

For the grown-ups

This is how we wish we’d discovered science, geography, history and the world.

Not as chapters to memorise, but as places to go: a leaf you can mount in an apparatus, a river you can flood, a circuit you can break and fix, an atom you can build one proton at a time. Ploobia is made for ages 10 to 17 and shaped to sit alongside the IGCSE curriculum, so what a learner discovers here is what they will meet in the exam room later — except they will have watched it move, measured it, and argued about it first.

Free to enter

The first regions and the whole Explorer level are free forever. Nothing to install, and no account needed to start.

Made to be measured

Every region ends in evidence — a prediction, readings, a graph and a written conclusion — and the family home shows what was actually done.

One world, three levels

The same simulations serve a ten-year-old and a seventeen-year-old; the vocabulary, mathematics and rigour grow with the learner.

Ploobia is early and honest about it: five regions are open today, more are being drawn, and the map will keep growing. If you can chip in, it pays for hosting and the next region. If you can’t, please don’t — that is exactly who this is for.
